How to read this chart

Bright bandsPrices where many leveraged positions would be force-closed. Brighter means more money at stake.
White lineThe coin's price over the selected range.
White circlesLiquidations Kraken actually recorded that hour. Bigger circle, bigger dollar amount.
Dashed lineToday's price, carried across so you can compare it with every band.
Side barsTeal: shorts that get liquidated if price rises. Red: longs that get liquidated if price falls.
Click to test a priceClick or tap the chart. You'll see how much would be liquidated if price moved there.
Liquidation
When a leveraged trader's losses eat their collateral, the exchange closes the position for them. That forced buying or selling can push price further.
Open interest
The total value of futures positions still open. More open interest means more positions that could be liquidated.
Leverage (10×, 100×)
Borrowing to make a position bigger. At 10× a move of about 10% against you wipes the position out; at 100×, about 1%.
Funding
A fee perpetual futures traders pay each other. Positive means longs pay shorts (more longs open); the map uses it to tilt its long/short estimate.
Long / short
Longs profit when price rises and are liquidated below the price. Shorts profit when price falls and are liquidated above it.

This is a model. Open interest, funding and recorded liquidations are real exchange data, collected hourly from Kraken and Crypto.com. Which side each position is on and how much leverage it uses are estimates (funding tilts the side split). It describes the market; it is not a trading signal or financial advice.

When Kraken data is loaded, each hour's rise in open interest on the Kraken perpetual is placed as new positions at that hour's price, split between longs and shorts using the funding rate (50/50 when funding is neutral) across the selected leverage tiers (0.5% maintenance margin). Open interest is Kraken's perpetuals plus Crypto.com's (hourly snapshots from Oct 3; BNB is Kraken only). Between hourly updates, the latest price and Crypto.com open interest come live from Crypto.com every 30 seconds, so the price line, distances and "near" flags stay current. Falling open interest closes positions proportionally, and a level is cleared once price trades through it. The last 30 days use hourly data; older history on the 90d view uses 4-hour data. White circles are liquidations Kraken actually recorded, sized by dollar volume. Long/short split and leverage are assumptions; open interest and liquidations are real. Not financial advice.
